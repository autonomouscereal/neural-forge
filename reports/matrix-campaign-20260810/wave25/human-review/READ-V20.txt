**Why Earth Has Seasons (and Why Summer Doesn’t Happen Because of Earth’s Closest Proximity to the Sun):**

1. **The Sun’s Energy and Light**:  
   Earth’s seasons are caused by the Sun’s energy reaching different parts of the planet. The tilt of Earth’s axis (about 23.5 degrees) means sunlight changes direction and intensity at different times of the year. This variation in sunlight exposure creates seasons.

2. **Tilt and Angle**:  
   Earth’s orbit around the Sun is tilted at about 23.5 degrees, so sunlight comes at different angles depending on where it lands on the planet. This tilt is what makes seasons happen.

3. **Tidal Heating**:  
   The Sun’s gravity also causes tides, which are stronger on the side of Earth facing the Sun. This extra energy from tides adds to the heating of the planet’s surface, making it warmer in summer.

4. **Atmospheric and Oceanic Changes**:  
   The tilt and tilt also affect how much sunlight is blocked by the atmosphere and ocean. For example, the Northern and Southern hemispheres receive more direct sunlight, while the tropics receive less. This variation in sunlight intensity leads to different temperatures and weather patterns.

5. **Long-Term Climate Patches**:  
   Over time, Earth’s tilt and tilt change gradually, causing seasons to shift. This process is called **atmospheric tilting**, which is a natural part of Earth’s climate system.

**Why Summer Doesn’t Happen Because of Earth’s Closest Proximity to the Sun**:  
While Earth’s distance from the Sun does affect the amount of sunlight received, the **tidal heating** from the Sun’s gravity is the primary driver of summer. The Sun’s gravity creates tides, which add extra heat to the planet’s surface, making it warmer in summer. This is why the Northern and Southern hemispheres experience more extreme temperatures compared to the tropics.

In short, Earth’s tilt and tilt, combined with the Sun’s energy, create the seasons we experience. The closer Earth is to the Sun, the more intense the heating, leading to the seasons we see!

Here's a Python function that **removes duplicates while preserving the first-seen order**, using a set to track seen items and a list to maintain the order:

```python
def unique_in_order(items):
    seen = set()
    unique_items = []

    for item in items:
        if item not in seen:
            seen.additem()
            unique_items.append(item)

    return unique_items
```

### Time Complexity:
- **O(n)**, where `n` is the number of items in the input list. Each item is processed once, and set operations (addition and membership checks) are average O(1).

### Short Explanation:
- The function iterates through each item in the input list.
- It checks if the item is already in the `set` (to detect duplication).
- If not, it adds the item to the `set` and appends it to the `list` of unique items.

This approach ensures that the order of first-seen items is maintained while removing any repeated items.
